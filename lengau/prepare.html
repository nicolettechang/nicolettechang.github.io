Prepare Lengau data for download

You are preparing a download list of folders and files to save from oblivion. But before you download, there are a few things to consider. Most folders come down as they are. Only folders you mark as a tarball are packed on Lengau first: a tarball is a single file that contains a folder and all its contents.

The commands below use your details.

Your computer  ·  Lengau

How should it travel?

You've decided how much of each folder to keep. Now choose how it gets to your drive. This can save you a lot of time. You can specify which method to use for each folder in your download list.

  1. 1 Arrives ready to use

    As it is rsync

    Downloads file by file, links left out. Slow for thousands of small files, and it carries on where it stopped if the VPN drops unlike scp.

    Best for folders of big files, such as model output and restarts. Nothing to do on Lengau first.

    On your drive: ready to use as soon as it lands.

  2. 2 Arrives ready to use

    Stream tar over ssh

    Lengau packs the folder as it sends it and your computer unpacks it as it arrives, so it is as fast as a tarball, but takes no space or job on Lengau, and keeps its links. If the VPN drops, doesn't start again: the rsync command under it copies only the files that are missing.

    For folders of thousands of small files: code, logs, many short runs.

    On your drive: ready to use as soon as it lands.

  3. 3 pack on lengau - unpack before use

    Tarball .tar

    You pack your files on Lengau first, one file per folder, so it takes a job and as much free space in your scratch as the folder. Then use rsync to copy the tarball to your drive.

    For a huge folder of small files on a connection that keeps dropping, or if you want your files archived.

    On your drive: unpack it before use, so you need room for it twice. tar xf can pull out a single file.

  4. 4 pack on lengau - unpack before use

    Compressed tarball .tar.gz

    You pack your folders on Lengau first, so it takes a job and as much free space in your scratch as the folder. Then use rsync to copy the tarball to your drive.

    Worth it for text: logs, code, CSV. NetCDF barely shrinks, and packing a big folder takes hours.

    On your drive: unpack it before use too, and getting at one file means reading through the whole tarball.
